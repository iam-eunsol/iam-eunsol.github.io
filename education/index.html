My path

From designing for people
to studying how they decide

I have always been drawn to how people use technology. I started in industrial design at KAIST, concentrating in human–computer interaction, where I learned to look at technology from the user’s side. My master’s in management engineering added an economic lens, as I studied how information technology reshapes markets, from media to energy. At NYU Stern, behavioral economics brought the two together. It gives me a benchmark for what a good decision looks like and theories for why people’s choices depart from it, which is how I now study people deciding with AI.
